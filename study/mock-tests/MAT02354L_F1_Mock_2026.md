## Questions — stop here until you have attempted them

### 1. Conditional probability and Bayes (4 points; ~24 minutes)
A shop receives 60% of its sensors from supplier A and 40% from supplier B. A sensor from A is defective with probability 0.02; one from B is defective with probability 0.05. A purchased sensor is randomly selected.
1. Compute the probability that it is defective, D. (1 point)
2. Given that it is defective, compute the probability that it came from B. (1 point)
3. Find the probability that it came from A and is not defective. (1 point)
4. Suppose three purchases are independent and follow the same supplier mix. Find the probability that none is defective. State why independence matters. (1 point)

### 2. Discrete joint probability (4 points; ~24 minutes)
The joint PMF of X (rows) and Y (columns) is:

| X\\Y | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.10 |
| 1 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 0.30 |

1. Check that this is a valid PMF and compute the marginal PMF of Y. (1 point)
2. Find P(X=1 | Y=2). (1 point)
3. Calculate E[X], E[Y], and E[XY]. (1 point)
4. Calculate Cov(X,Y). Explain what the sign means. (1 point)

### 3. Pick the correct count model (4 points; ~24 minutes)
1. Let X ~ Binomial(n=12,p=0.2). Find P(X=3), E[X], and Var(X). Show the formula before substituting. (2 points)
2. A box contains 20 objects, 5 marked. Four are drawn *without replacement*. Find the probability of exactly one marked object. Name the distribution and explain why binomial would not be exact. (2 points)

### 4. A continuous density (4 points; ~24 minutes)
The random variable T has density f(t)=2t for 0<t<1 and 0 elsewhere.
1. Verify that f is a valid PDF. (1 point)
2. Derive F(t) for all real t. (1 point)
3. Compute P(T>0.5). (1 point)
4. Compute E[T] and Var(T). (1 point)

### 5. Exponential and Normal distributions (4 points; ~24 minutes)
1. A component lifetime X is exponential with **rate** λ=0.5 per hour. Find P(X>3) and P(X>8 | X>5). State the property used. (2 points)
2. A measurement W is normal with mean 100 and standard deviation 15. Find P(W≤115) and P(85<W<115). Express answers using the standard normal CDF Φ, then give approximations. (2 points)

## Fully worked answer key

### 1. Bayes
1. Law of total probability: P(D)=P(D|A)P(A)+P(D|B)P(B)=0.02(0.6)+0.05(0.4)=**0.032**.
2. Bayes: P(B|D)=P(D|B)P(B)/P(D)=0.05(0.4)/0.032=**0.625**.
3. P(A and not D)=0.6(1−0.02)=**0.588**.
4. Per-purchase P(not D)=1−0.032=0.968. Independent purchases give **0.968³≈0.90704**. Without independence, multiplying three marginal probabilities is unjustified.

### 2. Joint PMF
1. All entries are nonnegative and add to 1. Y marginal: P(Y=0)=0.30, P(Y=1)=0.30, P(Y=2)=0.40.
2. P(X=1|Y=2)=0.30/0.40=**0.75**.
3. P(X=1)=0.60, so **E[X]=0.6**. E[Y]=0(0.3)+1(0.3)+2(0.4)=**1.1**. E[XY]=(1)(1)(0.1)+(1)(2)(0.3)=**0.7**.
4. Cov(X,Y)=E[XY]−E[X]E[Y]=0.7−0.6(1.1)=**0.04**. A small positive covariance indicates a weak positive linear tendency in these units, not independence or causality.

### 3. Count models
1. P(X=3)=C(12,3) (0.2)³(0.8)⁹=**0.23622**. E[X]=np=**2.4**; Var(X)=np(1−p)=**1.92**.
2. Hypergeometric: P(K=1)=C(5,1)C(15,3)/C(20,4)=2275/4845≈**0.46956**. Because draws are without replacement, success probabilities change after draws; they are not independent Bernoulli trials.

### 4. Continuous density
1. f(t)≥0 and ∫ from 0 to 1 of 2t dt=[t²] from 0 to 1=**1**.
2. **F(t)=0 for t≤0; t² for 0<t<1; 1 for t≥1**.
3. P(T>0.5)=1−F(0.5)=1−0.25=**0.75**.
4. E[T]=∫₀¹ t(2t) dt=2/3. E[T²]=∫₀¹ t²(2t) dt=1/2. **Var(T)=1/2−(2/3)²=1/18≈0.05556**.

### 5. Continuous probability models
1. Exponential with rate λ: P(X>x)=exp(−λx). P(X>3)=**exp(−1.5)≈0.22313**. Memorylessness: P(X>8|X>5)=P(X>3)=**exp(−1.5)≈0.22313**.
2. Standardise Z=(W−100)/15. P(W≤115)=Φ(1)≈**0.8413**. P(85<W<115)=Φ(1)−Φ(−1)≈**0.6827**. Normal probabilities come from standard normal tables, not the PDF height itself.

## Extra revision, not counted toward 20 points
For Gamma(shape α=2, **rate** λ=0.5), M(t)=(1−t/λ)^(−α) for t<λ. Check M′(0)=α/λ=4 and Var=α/λ²=8. The excerpt uses rate notation; do not confuse it with scale β=1/λ=2. This topic appears in the posted Murteira excerpt, but its inclusion in the first test is **unconfirmed**.
