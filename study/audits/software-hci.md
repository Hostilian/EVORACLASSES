## Scope decisions

The active Software set excludes generic Singleton/Observer/Factory/MVC, SOLID, code-review detail, CI/CD, and detailed Scrum/Kanban material. Those details are absent from the four posted lectures. Scrum, Kanban, configuration management, mocks and TDD appear in the course programme but their detailed lessons are not yet posted in the supplied source set. General agile planning, reuse, testing and use cases remain where actually covered.

The active HCI set excludes generic recitations of Nielsen's ten heuristics, WCAG thresholds, Fitts/Hick formulas, Gestalt principles, and other later-module details. These topics are named in the programme and some are required for later project milestones, but the only detailed posted lecture is introductory. The passing notes preserve that future-work list without presenting unposted teaching as completed coverage. Required phase-1 artifacts receive concise explanatory definitions labelled as such in each source field.

Administrative deadlines and weights live in passing notes, rather than consuming repeated recall cards. Bibliography entries, author contact details, decorative photos, section-divider slides, and joke text are not card filler. Their relevant teaching point is retained where present.

## Accuracy and authority

The Software presentation explicitly supplies years, resolving the shorter Moodle assessment page's unstated years: tests 28 October and 9 December 2026; exams 8 and 22 January 2027.

For HCI, Aula 1 explicitly states no frequências, despite the course-introduction matrix's broader 'Exame / Frequência Individual' label. Both agree on 40% individual theory and 60% practical assessment. Aula 1 also states a minimum of 8/20 in each component and mandatory presentation participation.

The Gemini PDF's first page gives the project requirement and milestone date. Pages 2–7 explicitly contain AI proposals. Sample persona biographies, interview sample sizes, session lengths, questionnaire timings and feature suggestions are not treated as compulsory rules or observed facts.

Source-specific classroom illustrations are not promoted to universal facts. Examples include approximate cost proportions and the HCI slide's 50% cost illustration. The Software introduction's maintenance/development cost wording includes an apparent inconsistency; the card retains the supported economic point about expensive evolution rather than drilling a conflicting numeric rule.

Generated application questions are study exercises grounded in the source concepts, not actual past-paper questions. No past papers, marking schemes or detailed exam format were supplied. Coverage is complete for the posted source set, while exact examination scope and forthcoming lecture content remain unverified.
