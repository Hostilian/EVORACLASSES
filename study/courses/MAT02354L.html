Week 3: define what the variable counts

Before choosing a formula, define X, list its possible values and state the assumptions. A formula for successes in a fixed number of trials answers a different question from a formula for trials until a success.

Trials versus failures: the slides' geometric X starts at 1. If Y=X-1 counts failures, Y starts at 0 and has mean (1-p)/p; variance is unchanged. A failures-count negative binomial likewise differs from the trials-count convention shown here.

Sums and approximations

  • Independent binomials with the same p add their n values.
  • Independent negative binomials with the same p and trials-count convention add their required-success counts.
  • Independent Poisson variables add their λ values.
  • Week 3's practical binomial-to-Poisson guideline is n>20 and p≤0.05; take λ=np and label the result approximate.
  • The slides suggest binomial approximation to hypergeometric when n<0.1 N. The exact model still accounts for draws without replacement.

Read the plots

Binomial p=0.5 is symmetric. Small p concentrates successes near zero; large p near n. At fixed p, increasing n moves the mean np. A larger geometric p gives more probability to early successes.

The discrete uniform CDF is a staircase whose jumps are 1/N. A PMF height is a point probability; a continuous PDF height is not.

Murteira: moments and continuous models

MGF: M(t)=E[etX] must be finite on an interval around zero. Use a sum for a discrete X and an integral over the support for a continuous X. M(0)=1, M′(0)=E[X], M″(0)=E[X²], and Var(X)=M″(0)-[M′(0)]². The kth derivative at zero gives a raw moment, not automatically a central moment. Existence of all moments alone does not guarantee an MGF.

For independent variables the MGF of their sum is the product. The excerpt derives geometric M(t)=pet/(1-(1-p)et), t<-ln(1-p), and differentiates it to recover the mean and variance.

Fresh practice: attempt before checking

These original study problems rehearse the reviewed methods. They are not an actual past paper or assessed submission. Use one in a budgeted study block; write the steps, record the real error and try a changed problem later.

  1. Fixed trials: n=10 independent trials, p=0.3. Find P(exactly two successes), E[X] and Var(X); explain the model.

    Check reasoning

    Binomial: C(10,2)0.3²0.7⁸≈0.2335; mean=3; variance=2.1. Geometric would count trials until a success instead.

  2. Trial counting: independent trials, p=0.25. Find P(first success on trial 4) and P(third success on trial 5).

    Check reasoning

    First: 0.75³× 0.25=0.10546875. Third: choose two successes among the first four, then force a success on trial five: C(4,2)0.25³0.75²=0.052734375. Expected trial counts are 4 and 12; expected failure counts are 3 and 9.

  3. Approximation: X~Binomial(100,0.02). Approximate P(X=0) and compare with its exact value.

    Check reasoning

    The slides' guideline holds; λ=np=2. Poisson gives e⁻²≈0.1353; exact binomial gives 0.98¹⁰⁰≈0.1326. They are close, not identical; name the approximation.

  4. No replacement: 20 items contain 5 marked items. Draw 4 without replacement. Find P(exactly one marked), mean and variance.

    Check reasoning

    Hypergeometric: C(5,1)C(15,3)/C(20,4)=2275/4845≈0.4696. Mean=1; variance=4 × 0.25 × 0.75 × 16/19=12/19≈0.6316. A binomial calculation would ignore dependence and the finite-population factor.

  5. Categories: three independent observations have category probabilities 0.2, 0.3, 0.5. Find P(one in each) and Cov(XA,XB).

    Check reasoning

    Multinomial: 3!× 0.2 × 0.3 × 0.5=0.18. Cov(XA,XB)=-3 × 0.2 × 0.3=-0.18. Counts are dependent because their sum is fixed.

  6. MGF moments: gamma shape α=2, rate λ=0.5. Use the MGF to derive mean and variance.

    Check reasoning

    M(t)=(1-t/λ)⁻α. M′(0)=α/λ=4 and M″(0)=α(α+1)/λ²=24. Variance=24-4²=8. The stated rate 0.5 corresponds to scale 2, not scale 0.5.

  7. Lifetimes: four independent components each have exponential rate 0.2/hour. The system fails at the first failure. Find its mean lifetime. For one component alive at five hours, find P(survive three more).

    Check reasoning

    The minimum has rate 4 × 0.2=0.8/hour, so mean=1.25 hours. Memorylessness gives P(X>8 | X>5)=P(X>3)=e⁻⁰·⁶≈0.5488. The minimum result requires independence and equal rates.

  8. Parameters: find mean and variance for Beta(2,3). Separately find covariance for a bivariate normal with σx=2, σy=3, ρ=-0.5.

    Check reasoning

    Beta mean=2/5=0.4; variance=6/(25 × 6)=0.04. Covariance=(-0.5)× 2 × 3=-3. Negative correlation means higher X tends to accompany lower Y. Correlation is dimensionless; covariance depends on units. Here |ρ|<1.

Catch these errors: wrong support; trials/failures confusion; treating no-replacement draws as independent; ignoring fixed count totals; mistaking E[X²] for variance; swapping rate and scale; calling an approximation exact.
